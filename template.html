    How the model works

    The country is a grid of 1 km cells on a real elevation model. Each year the model knows how much of each land use the country should have, and picks which cells change. It scores them by slope, altitude, rain shadow, distance to towns that exist that year, what their neighbours already are, and some noise. Forest clears outward from towns and existing farmland, dairy clusters on flat, wetter land, and pine clusters on pumice and hill country.

    From 1996 the map is the Land Cover Database: each cell takes the most common LCDB class inside it at each survey (1996/97, 2001/02, 2008/09, 2012/13, 2018/19, 2023/24), and changes between surveys are spread across the years in between. Before 1996 every cell may only move towards the class LCDB records for it in 1996. The model chooses when that happened and by what route, for example forest to pasture to scrub.

    What it is anchored to

    • LCDB v6.0 land cover at six dates from 1996 to 2023, majority class per 1 km cell.
    • Natural cover still present in 1996 (forest, tussock, wetland, alpine) is assumed present in 1840.
    • About 53% of the land in native forest in 1840, about a quarter today.
    • Wetland in 1840 about ten times today's mapped extent.
    • Terrain from a 1-arcsecond DEM interpolated from LINZ topographic contours; coastline from LINZ Topo50.
    • Town founding dates and rough population history for 52 settlements.

    Read it with care

    The years before 1996 are a reconstruction, not a dataset. National totals between 1840 and 1996 follow rough historical estimates, and individual cells will often be wrong about timing. The 1840 map fills everything that was developed by 1996 using rules about rain shadow, wetland zones and Māori settlement, so it is a plausible pattern, not a survey.

    Even the observed years are summarised: each cell shows the dominant LCDB class, so small or narrow features such as wetlands, towns and shelterbelts are undercounted. Lakes are shown from 1840, including hydro lakes formed later. The split between dairy and sheep & beef is modelled throughout, and 2024–2025 repeats the 2023/24 map.
