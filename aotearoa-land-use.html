    How the model works

    The country is a grid of 2.5 km cells. In 1840 each cell starts as native forest, scrub and fernland, tussock, wetland or alpine, ranked by a rough terrain model, rainfall shadow and distance from Māori settlement.

    Each year the model knows how much of each land use the country should have. It then picks which cells change, scoring them by slope, altitude, dryness, distance to towns that exist that year, what their neighbours already are, and some noise. Forest clears outward from towns and existing farmland, dairy clusters on flat wetter land, pine clusters on pumice and hill country.

    What it is anchored to

    • About half of the land in forest in 1840, and about a quarter today.
    • LUCAS and LCDB totals for pasture, plantation, cropping and urban land since 1990.
    • Wetland reduced to about a tenth of its 1840 extent.
    • Town founding dates and rough population history for 54 settlements.
    • Founding years of national and forest parks, which stop clearance inside them.

    Read it with care

    This is a sketch, not a dataset. Terrain is hand-approximated from mountain ranges, not from a real elevation model, and class totals between survey years are interpolated. Individual cells will often be wrong; the broad sequence and pattern are what the model shows.
